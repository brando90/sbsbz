## Funding routes and current requirements

The Student Arts Grant deadline for Cycle A is **10-15-2026 at 4 p.m. Pacific**. Awards range from $250 to $3,500, are limited to one per group each academic year, and cannot exceed the group contribution. The project needs an enrolled undergraduate/co-term lead, an active registered student organization and access for all Stanford students. Social parties are excluded. Plan at least 4–6 weeks ahead. The live form lists this club. [Official guidance](https://arts.stanford.edu/office-of-the-vice-president-for-the-arts/student-grants/how-to-apply/) · [Application](https://airtable.com/app9hvZ3nSMmxN7LN/pageR5UUvYlXmA99L/form).

The Billie Achilles Fund may provide up to $500 per event and $1,000 per group per academic year. It requires a registered organization with an international focus and a genuinely intercultural event that is free and open to the whole Stanford community. Apply at least eight weeks ahead; primarily social events and alcohol are excluded. The club must confirm its fit and remaining yearly allowance. [Official guidance](https://bechtel.stanford.edu/connect-campus/programs-events/space-reservations/billieachillesfund) · [Application](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSdeXWFYVnGuFboRVpKa2nDcbiyJW_3fc5H8GaeHrc27aJ86Qw/viewform).

The arts expense guide excludes food, class costs, payments to Stanford affiliates and expenses incurred before the award. Outside speaker/performer honoraria are only sometimes eligible. Ask the program to evaluate the actual guest-artist duties; do not relabel ordinary teaching fees. Venue services, sound rental and printing may be suitable. [Expense guide](https://airtable.com/app9hvZ3nSMmxN7LN/shrhpkBukw6BBAbTY).

## Proposed program for the club to adopt

**Title:** Bachata in Context: Rhythm, Movement and Cultural Exchange

**Host:** Stanford Bachata Sensual and Brazilian Zouk (SBSBZ).

**Proposed format:** one free, two-hour workshop with cultural context, musical listening, beginner-accessible movement, guided practice and student discussion. No partner or prior dance experience required. Invite participation from across Stanford; provide a way to observe or participate without partner contact.

| Segment | Proposed minutes | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Welcome, access options and partner consent | 10 | Explain choices, comfort and respectful participation. |
| Cultural context and guided listening | 20 | Introduce Dominican bachata and distinguish its heritage from later adaptations. |
| Artist demonstration and fundamentals | 45 | Connect musical rhythm to accessible movement; support different experience levels. |
| Guided student practice | 30 | Apply skills with instructor feedback; optional partner changes. |
| Reflection and short feedback | 15 | Identify learning and encourage continued participation. |
| Total | 120 | Educational program, with student participation throughout. |

This is an authored design, not an already approved schedule. An appropriately qualified presenter must shape and substantiate the cultural content. Dominican bachata's music and dance were recognized in 2019 by the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO); that designation does not establish that every later dance style has the same recognition. [Heritage source](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/music-and-dance-of-dominican-bachata-01514).

**Timing recommendation:** choose a date after the relevant award and transfer timeline, room availability and academic calendar are checked. Winter quarter may provide more planning room, but verify the chosen grant cycle's event window.

### Project proposal — draft

We propose Bachata in Context to make participatory dance education accessible while connecting movement with the music and cultural traditions that give it meaning. The project extends our club's commitment to free dance learning and careful technique. Its purpose is to help students experience bachata as an expressive practice and understand its Dominican roots alongside later adaptations.

The two-hour program will combine guided listening, cultural context, an artist demonstration, beginner-accessible movement and facilitated student practice. A closing discussion will invite participants to explain what they learned about rhythm, communication and cultural context. Students may attend without a partner, choose their dance role, decline partner changes or observe. We will communicate access information and accommodation contacts in advance.

[Undergraduate/co-term lead] will direct the project and own the schedule and final report. [Registered officer] will oversee the budget and supporting records; [logistics lead] will manage room permission and setup; [outreach/check-in lead] will coordinate publicity and attendance. These roles will be agreed by the club leadership and recorded with deadlines before submission. Our proposed accountability process is a shared task list, weekly check-ins, written confirmation of bookings and quotations, and an expense record reviewed by the financial officer. Artist selection will consider relevant expertise, ability to teach beginners, cultural knowledge, availability and cost.

At least two weeks before the event, we will release the final publicity and registration information. On [date], student volunteers will manage check-in and support the workshop. The intercultural component is built into the program: participants will connect music with its cultural context and exchange observations about how dance communicates across backgrounds. ## Documentation and reporting plan — proposed

Use one private check-in record per event: event title/date, attendee name, attendance confirmation, and optional affiliation category where needed for the funder. Tell participants the list supports grant reporting. Keep marketing opt-in separate; do not collect birthdates, identification numbers, immigration status or unrelated personal data. Restrict the named list to authorized organizers and the relevant funder.

Record total attendees and undergraduate/graduate/other categories when legitimately required, plus repeat versus first-time participation if voluntarily supplied. Collect an optional exit response: one cultural insight, confidence applying a rhythm or movement, and a suggestion. Report observed totals and response counts; do not fabricate improvements or treat registrations as attendance. Obtain consent before using identifiable photos.

Maintain the award letter, approved budget, venue confirmation, quotations, invoices, payment records, publicity and event report together. Assign a named report owner before submission, with a backup who remains enrolled/available through the leadership transition.
